Phonics for kids: hearing beginning, middle and end sounds

Before children can read a word, they need to hear the sounds inside it. Letter names and letter sounds first, then beginning, ending and middle sounds, then picture-to-letter reading. This page gives that order, sound games you can play anywhere, and how Lumi Letters Pro practises each step.

The phonics path, step by step

StepWhat to practiseExample
1. Letter name and soundB is called “bee” and says /b/ball, bus
2. Beginning soundWhat sound does the word start with?sun starts with /s/
3. Ending soundWhat sound does it end with?cat ends with /t/
4. Middle soundThe vowel in the middlecat and cut differ in the middle
5. Picture phonicsMatch a picture to its letterapple → A

These are general suggestions. When your child is unsure, step back one level instead of pushing ahead.

Ten-minute sound games

  • Sound hunt: “Find something in the house that starts with /b/.” Ball, book, bed.
  • Last sound: say a word slowly and ask which sound comes at the end.
  • Stretch the middle: say “c-aaa-t” and ask which sound you stretched.
  • Big and small: write a few capitals and ask your child to find the lowercase partners.
  • What comes next: write A B _ D and ask which letter is missing.

Keep sounds short and clean: /m/ is “mmm”, not “muh”. Extra vowels make blending harder later. Stop while it is still fun.

Frequently asked questions

Should I teach letter names or letter sounds first?

Teach them together. The name helps children talk about letters; the sound is what they use to read and spell.

Why practise beginning, middle and end sounds?

Hearing each sound in a word is the groundwork for blending sounds into words, one part of getting ready to read.
